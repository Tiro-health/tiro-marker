Comprehensive Genetics/Cardiology Consultation

Date: February 10, 2026

Patient: Marcus Thompson, 24-year-old male

Referring Physician: Dr. Sarah Chen, Internal Medicine

Consultant: Dr. Elizabeth Ramos, MD, PhD - Clinical Genetics & Cardiovascular Medicine

Reason for Consultation

Evaluation for suspected hereditary connective tissue disorder with aortopathy and multisystem involvement. Patient referred following identification of aortic root dilatation on routine echocardiography performed for evaluation of mitral valve prolapse murmur.

History of Present Illness

This 24-year-old male of tall stature (198 cm) with arm span exceeding height (arm span-to-height ratio 1.08) presents with progressive aortic root dilatation. The patient has noted decreased visual acuity since adolescence, requiring high-power myopic correction. He reports a prior episode of spontaneous pneumothorax at age 19, managed conservatively with chest tube drainage.

The patient denies exertional chest pain, syncope, or palpitations. He does endorse occasional dyspnea with strenuous activity. No history of aortic dissection. Physical activity has been self-limited per prior recommendations.

Family History

Significant for autosomal dominant inheritance pattern. Father deceased at age 42 from acute aortic dissection. Paternal grandfather with similar habitus, died suddenly at age 48 (presumed cardiac). One affected sibling (sister, age 22) with confirmed diagnosis. Three first-degree relatives affected in total. Maternal family history unremarkable. Family history positive for sudden cardiac death.

Cardiovascular Examination and Imaging

Transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) demonstrates aortic root diameter at the sinuses of Valsalva measuring 4.8 cm (Z-score +3.2, significantly above the +2.0 threshold). Ascending aorta measures 3.6 cm, within normal limits. No evidence of aortic dissection on current imaging. Mitral valve demonstrates myxomatous degeneration with bileaflet prolapse and moderate mitral regurgitation. Mild tricuspid regurgitation present. Left ventricular ejection fraction preserved at 60%.

Cardiac MRI with contrast confirms echocardiographic findings. No intramural hematoma or penetrating atherosclerotic ulcer identified. Flow analysis demonstrates no significant aortic regurgitation at this time.

Ophthalmologic Evaluation

Comprehensive examination by neuro-ophthalmology reveals bilateral ectopia lentis with superotemporal displacement of the crystalline lens (characteristic direction for this syndrome, distinguishing from homocystinuria which demonstrates inferonasal displacement). High myopia present bilaterally with spherical equivalent of -8.5 diopters OD and -8.0 diopters OS. Dilated fundus examination demonstrates peripheral lattice degeneration in both eyes, concerning for increased retinal detachment risk. No evidence of acute retinal pathology. Patient has been counseled regarding symptoms of retinal detachment and urgency of evaluation if symptoms develop.

Musculoskeletal Assessment

Physical examination demonstrates characteristic marfanoid habitus with dolichostenomelia. Upper segment to lower segment ratio is 0.82 (reduced, confirming disproportionate limb length). Walker-Murdoch sign (wrist sign) positive bilaterally - thumb and fifth finger overlap when encircling contralateral wrist. Steinberg sign (thumb sign) positive - entire distal phalanx of thumb extends beyond ulnar border of palm when thumb is enclosed in fist.

Arachnodactyly present with elongated, slender digits. Significant joint hypermobility with Beighton score of 7/9. Pes planus (flat feet) present bilaterally with hindfoot valgus. Pectus excavatum noted with sternal depression causing mild restrictive physiology. CT chest demonstrates Haller index of 3.8, indicating severe pectus deformity (normal <2.5, surgical threshold >3.25).

Spinal radiographs reveal thoracolumbar scoliosis with Cobb angle measuring 28 degrees, predominantly dextroscoliosis in the thoracic spine. No spondylolisthesis identified.

Neuroimaging

MRI of the lumbosacral spine demonstrates dural ectasia with widening of the spinal canal at L5-S1, anterior meningoceles present, and scalloping of the posterior vertebral bodies. These findings contribute to the systemic score for diagnostic criteria.

Genetic Testing Results

Next-generation sequencing panel for hereditary aortopathy genes returned positive. A pathogenic heterozygous missense variant was identified in the FBN1 gene: c.4082G>A (p.Cys1361Tyr), located in exon 33 within a calcium-binding EGF-like domain. This cysteine substitution disrupts a critical disulfide bond in the fibrillin-1 protein, consistent with classic disease phenotype. This variant is classified as pathogenic (ACMG criteria: PS3, PM1, PM2, PP3, PP5). Cascade genetic testing recommended for at-risk family members.

Revised Ghent Criteria Assessment

Systemic score calculation: Wrist AND thumb sign (+3), Pectus excavatum requiring surgery (+2), Reduced US/LS AND increased arm span/height (+1), Scoliosis >20 degrees (+1), Pes planus (+1), Dural ectasia (+2) = Total systemic score: 9 points (threshold >/=7 is highly suggestive).

Diagnostic assessment: Patient meets revised Ghent criteria for Marfan syndrome through multiple pathways: (1) Aortic root Z-score >/=2 PLUS pathogenic FBN1 variant; (2) Aortic root Z-score >/=2 PLUS ectopia lentis; (3) Systemic score >/=7 PLUS pathogenic FBN1 variant.

Current Medical Management

Patient currently maintained on atenolol 50mg twice daily for heart rate control and reduction of aortic wall stress (dP/dt reduction). Additionally, losartan 100mg daily has been initiated based on evidence suggesting TGF-beta pathway modulation may attenuate aortic root growth independent of hemodynamic effects. Blood pressure target <120/80 mmHg achieved on current regimen.

Surgical Planning Discussion

Given progressive aortic root dilatation approaching surgical threshold (5.0 cm or rapid expansion >0.5 cm/year), prophylactic aortic root replacement is being discussed. Options include Bentall procedure with mechanical composite graft (requiring lifelong anticoagulation with target INR 2.5-3.5) versus valve-sparing David procedure if aortic valve morphology permits. No previous Bentall procedure or other cardiac surgery has been performed. Patient preference is for valve-sparing approach to avoid anticoagulation requirements given young age and desire for future athletic activity. Multi-disciplinary heart team conference scheduled to finalize surgical approach.

Recommendations

1. Continue current medical therapy with beta-blocker and ARB combination
2. Serial echocardiographic surveillance every 6 months given approaching surgical threshold
3. Activity restriction: avoid contact sports, isometric exercise, and activities with Valsalva maneuver
4. Genetic counseling for family members; cascade testing offered
5. Annual ophthalmologic examination with dilated fundus exam
6. Endocarditis prophylaxis not routinely recommended per current guidelines given MVP without significant regurgitation
7. Pre-conception genetic counseling recommended given autosomal dominant inheritance (50% transmission risk)

Diagnosis

Marfan syndrome (OMIM #154700), genetically confirmed, with aortic root dilatation, mitral valve prolapse, ectopia lentis, and characteristic skeletal manifestations. Meets revised Ghent diagnostic criteria.
